vJEPA vs vMAE on The Well — probing sweep

Does latent prediction encode more physics than pixel reconstruction?

A lean, linear-probe-only sweep across all 3 trained dataset pairs — broadened physical quantities (pooled and per-token), regime probing, and a sliding-window forecast test — run to check whether the JEPA/MAE asymmetry found earlier is a general trend or a one-off.

3 datasets 2 objectives 13 layers probed each linear ridge probes only single seed

Executive summary

Four findings

  • Confirmed The relational-quantity reversal holds and sharpens at the per-token level: MAE retains velocity–order-tensor coupling far better than JEPA on active_matter and shear_flow, while JEPA is the one that builds up buoyancy–flow coupling on rayleigh_benard — MAE stays near-zero there throughout depth. Two independent quantities on rayleigh_benard now confirm the reversal, not one.
  • New Both objectives strongly encode which physical regime they're looking at (Reynolds/Schmidt, Rayleigh/Prandtl, activity/alignment) — R² mostly 0.6–1.0, shuffled-label controls collapse to negative. This is essentially objective-agnostic: not a JEPA/MAE story.
  • New On short-horizon forecasting, JEPA's predictor rarely beats a naive "assume nothing changes" baseline, and MAE's pixel-space forecast is substantially worse still — often failing outright. Neither model does much genuine forecasting yet at this scale/horizon.
  • Caveat Pooled (whole-clip-averaged) vorticity and divergence are close to meaningless by construction — a periodic domain's curl/divergence integrates to ≈0 — producing negative or undefined R² regardless of what the encoder knows. Always check the per-token version before trusting a pooled negative result.

Read on the mechanism On active_matter/shear_flow, JEPA starts roughly level with MAE near the input and then actively discards the relational signal with depth. On rayleigh_benard, it's the opposite shape: JEPA builds up the buoyancy-coupling representation through the first ~7 layers before a late partial decay, while MAE never picks it up at all. The candidate explanation from the earlier deep-dive still holds: rayleigh_benard's buoyancy-velocity coupling is the variable that drives the system's own dynamics (convection is the buoyancy force acting on velocity), so a latent-prediction objective that must forecast forward has direct incentive to represent it — where in the other two datasets the analogous coupling is comparatively incidental to what's being forecast.

What doesn't show the effect

Not every relational-looking quantity splits this cleanly. okubo_weiss (vorticity vs. strain balance) is close to a wash on rayleigh_benard at the per-token final layer (JEPA 0.819 vs MAE 0.895 — MAE slightly ahead, opposite direction from convective_flux). The reversal is real and now well-evidenced, but it isn't a universal law that "JEPA wins every relational quantity on rayleigh_benard" — treat it as a strong trend on the two quantities most tied to the system's own driving physics, not a blanket rule.

Finding 2 — regime probing

A new probe this sweep: does the pooled representation know which physical regime a trajectory belongs to (Reynolds/Schmidt number, Rayleigh/Prandtl number, activity/alignment strength) — parameters that are fixed per-trajectory and never shown to the model as labels. One feature vector per trajectory, ridge-probed against the (log-scaled where appropriate) regime value, with a shuffled-label control run alongside to catch leakage.

Finding 3 — sliding-window forecast probe

Also new: mask out the trailing 2 of 8 frames in a clip (a fixed, deterministic split — not the random tube-mask used in training) and ask each model's own trained prediction head to forecast them from real context, no retraining. JEPA forecasts in latent space (ridge-probed against real future physics, alongside a ceiling = probe on the real future's own features, and persistence = naively assume the future looks like the present). MAE forecasts in pixel space directly (reconstructed vs. real physics compared by correlation/R², with a proxy-sanity control isolating normalization-approximation error from genuine decoder failure). This is a forecast test with real pixels as input at every step — not autoregressive rollout.

Method notes

What to know before trusting these numbers

  • Pooled vorticity/divergence are near-meaningless. Under periodic boundary conditions the domain integral of curl and divergence is ≈0 by Stokes' theorem, so the whole-clip-pooled target has almost no variance — ridge regression on 4/5 cross-validation folds fits noise that doesn't generalize, producing reliably negative (sometimes undefined/NaN on rayleigh_benard, where the near-zero variance underflows exactly) R² regardless of what the encoder actually knows. The per-token version is the trustworthy one for these two quantities specifically.
  • shear_flow and rayleigh_benard ran at n_offsets=1, not 3. A memory ceiling on the compute pod (the derived-quantity computation materializes several full-clip-sized intermediate tensors, e.g. Okubo-Weiss needs four) meant the original 3-offsets-per-trajectory sampling used for active_matter and the historical deep-dive OOM'd on these two larger-resolution datasets. Results here use 1 window per trajectory instead — still real signal (consistent with the historical 3-offset pooled numbers where those overlap), but noisier and less averaged than active_matter's numbers. A follow-up pass should fix the streaming/memory handling in build_dataset() rather than reducing sample count.
  • Single seed, no significance testing. All R² values are point estimates from one 5-fold split. The reversal is well-evidenced by being consistent across two independent formulas on rayleigh_benard and two other datasets showing the opposite direction, but individual numbers (especially the smaller rayleigh_benard/shear_flow sample sizes above) should be read with real uncertainty bars in mind, not as exact.
  • "Rollout" here is not autoregressive. Every forecast step conditions on real trajectory pixels, never on the model's own prior prediction fed back in. It tests one-step-ahead forecasting capability, not compounding long-horizon rollout stability.

Where this leaves us

Recommended next steps

  1. Fund a deeper dive on the relational-quantity reversal. It's now the best-evidenced finding in the project — confirmed on 2 independent formulas on rayleigh_benard, opposite-direction on 2 other datasets, and sharper at the per-token level than pooled ever showed. Worth a multi-seed replication and, if resources allow, testing whether the effect holds at larger model scale.
  2. Fix build_dataset()'s memory handling so shear_flow and rayleigh_benard can be re-run at matching sample density (n_offsets=3) to remove the one real methodological asymmetry in this sweep — compute the derived quantities in batches rather than materializing them on the full stacked clip tensor.
  3. Don't chase pixel-space forecasting for MAE as-is. The proxy-normalization approach here is a reasonable stopgap for a lean probe, but the underlying result (MAE's forecast is close to non-functional on 2 of 3 datasets) is unlikely to be purely a normalization artifact given how consistently correlation/R² diverge. If forecasting quality matters to the research question, JEPA's latent-space forecast — while not yet beating persistence — is the more promising direction to push.
  4. Regime probing is not a JEPA/MAE differentiator — deprioritize further work here unless a specific downstream use case (e.g. regime classification as an application) makes it valuable in its own right.
  5. The "not every relational quantity reverses" caveat (Okubo-Weiss) is worth understanding, not dismissing — it may sharpen the mechanistic story (is it specifically the driving-force coupling that JEPA prioritizes, not relational quantities in general?) with a handful of well-chosen additional targets.
